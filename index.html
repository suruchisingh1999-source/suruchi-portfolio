Overview

A smart home that explains itself

Hearth is a concept for controlling home heating and cooling when the system decides things on its own. It is based on a 2025 Bosch white paper on agentic AI running on edge devices.

The problem

Automatic comfort systems tend to act first and explain never. A room cools at an odd hour, the schedule shifts, and the person living there feels they have lost control. Many also depend on the cloud, so comfort suffers when the internet drops.

The idea

Let the home work out its own plan, but show every decision as one plain sentence with a way back. Keep the deciding on a local hub, and give people hard limits the system can never cross.

Say whyEvery automatic action gets a reason in plain words and an Undo.
Work offlineDecisions run on the hub, so comfort does not depend on the internet.
Stay in chargeHard temperature bounds and a choice of how much the home may decide alone.

From the paper to the screen

Architecture, translated

The Bosch paper describes layers, agents and edge deployment. The design work was deciding what a person should see of that.

Explainability for the user

The paper's interface layer lists explanations of why an action was taken. Activity gives every automatic action one plain reason and an Undo.

Proactive, goal-based control

Instead of "turn on cooling at 8:00", the home holds a goal. Tonight draws that goal against the power price.

Edge resilience

Decisions run on a local hub and keep working offline. The hub screen says what still works and what never leaves the house.

Goals balanced by thresholds

The paper's eco-mode holds unless comfort is at risk. Limits turns that into hard temperature bounds and a choice of how much the home may decide.

Predictive maintenance

The boiler example becomes an outdoor AC unit working harder, shown as one quiet row on Home and one entry in Activity.

Overview

AI edits you can read like layers

Open Edit is a concept for an image editor where the AI does real work, but never in secret. Every change it makes lands as a named layer with a reason, an area on the picture, and a way back.

The problem

Generative edits arrive as one finished picture. Ask for golden hour and ten things change at once. You can't see what was touched, undo one part without losing the rest, or tell anyone what the AI did. Changes to people and words happen as quietly as changes to the sky.

The idea

Break every request into layers a person can read. Group them by the request that made them, show what each one was built on, and preview an Undo before it happens. The original photo is locked and never changed.

Every change is a layerNamed, explained, hideable, with an Undo. Grouped by the request that made it.
Undo shows the costHover Undo and the picture marks what would go, and what would need a check.
You set how freely it worksAsk first, tell me after, or handle quietly. People, text and your own edits can always ask.

    Design decisions

    Choices that make it trustworthy

    Layers, not a chat log

    A transcript scrolls away. Layers stay attached to the picture, so you can always find what changed and where.

    Group by request

    One request can make several changes. They sit in a group you can undo together, or one at a time.

    Fail without damage

    When the AI can't read the sign, nothing changes and it says so: your picture is exactly as it was.

    Say when it guesses

    Fixing "FRSH APLES" is a guess at what the sign should say, so the layer carries an "A guess" tag.

    Respect your own edits

    If an AI change would touch the jacket colour you set yourself, it stops and asks whose colour should win.

    Share what the AI did

    One button turns the layers into a plain list, so anyone receiving the picture can see what was made with AI.

    Built to be used

    Accessibility and small screens

    Circling has a typed twinEverything you can draw, you can also type below the picture. The circle button says so to screen readers.
    Handles and sliders take arrow keysArea handles move 8 pixels per key press. The compare divider is a real slider.
    Previews work on focusUndo and History previews appear on keyboard focus too, not only on hover.
    Same layers on a phoneThe list becomes a sheet under the image, using the same data. Reduced motion turns off the shimmer and fades.
